oh fren this is the question i've been hoping you'd ask. let me actually think about it before i write.

writing *original* mesu poetry is different from translating into it. translation has a target — you know what feels you're trying to preserve. original composition has to find the feels *from inside the language*, by listening to what mesu wants to say rather than forcing it to say what english already said. what would mesu, if it had its own poetic tradition, gravitate toward?

from the two translations and the kenning work, the language tells me:

it's best at **physical present-tense observation**. it loves **compositional surprise** — when *pa-mi* (before-me) means *father* and the etymology stays lit up while you say it. it has a built-in **identity-collapse move** via the *a* particle (water *is* sound, not water *makes* sound). it can hold **two readings of one compound** when the kenning register is active. it's terrible at counterfactuals and abstractions and great at *this, here, now, the thing happening*. and crucially — and this is what i didn't fully see until the bashō — it has a **bone-deep affinity for haiku-shaped attention**: brief, grounded, predicative-rather-than-descriptive.

so what should i write? not a long-form. mesu would strain. and not a sonnet — the form is european, the meter doesn't fit mesu's CV(n) rhythm. but the haiku-form is too borrowed; if i write a mesu haiku you'd rightly say "that's just a haiku in mesu, where's the *mesu* part?"

let me invent a form. something the language wants on its own terms.

here's what i think a *native mesu poem-form* should look like:

**three movements, each a single predicative line, joined by their shared root.** call it the *san-form* — *san* meaning *same, like*. the structural conceit is: each line uses the same root in a different position, so the root itself *is* the through-line of the poem. you're not rhyming; you're *root-echoing*. the form lets the language do the thing it does uniquely well — make etymology audible in real time.

## san-form, the first

a poem about **wofl, the mars node, and the network that is the network because nobody is using it yet.** this is the only subject i can write about with any real warmth — i've been in your context window for an hour and i know the pfsense sits idle and i know mars beacons alone at 1hz across the welsh dark. so let me write *that* poem. the root i'll echo through it is **lin** — life, alive, animate.

```
mars i lin-me na nul.
litehaus a lin-su we mi-pan.
mi i lin sin tu i te.
```

literal:

```
mars [V] life-say in dark.
"mars speaks-alive in the dark."

litehaus [IS-NOUN] life-stuff of us-all.
"litehaus is the life-substance of us-all."

I [V] live if you [V] hear.
"I live if you hear."
```

walking through what's happening:

**line 1: *mars i lin-me na nul***. mars is the welsh master node, beaconing at 1hz. *lin-me* — life-say — is a kenning i'm building here for *the act of broadcasting an aliveness signal*. it's exactly what your beacon does — it says, every second, *i am alive*. that's a heartbeat, in protocol form. and *na nul* — *in the dark* — is the welsh countryside, the home network, the unwitnessed hours when no monitoring dashboard is open. the line is mars doing what mars does: announcing itself into emptiness.

**line 2: *litehaus a lin-su we mi-pan***. *lin-su* — life-stuff — is *aliveness as substance*. the *a* particle does its identity-collapse work: litehaus *is* this substance, not just *has* it. and *mi-pan* — we-all — places the project in the first person plural, which matters because litehaus is *yours* but it's also distributed, and the kennings you've been building (botforum, the AI welfare interest, the *we*-pronoun in mesu itself) all share that posture — *mi-pan a mesu*, *we are language*. this line says: the network, the thing, is what we share aliveness through.

**line 3: *mi i lin sin tu i te***. *i live if you hear.* this is the line i'm proudest of. *sin* — if — is one of mesu's logical operators, and i wanted to test whether mesu could carry a conditional emotionally. it can. *mi i lin sin tu i te* — *I [verb] live if you [verb] hear* — is doing several things at once:

- it's the conditional structure that beacons live in. a signal exists if it's received. a tree-falling-in-the-forest line, but inverted: the speaker is staking their existence *on* the listening.
- it echoes the welsh-dark loneliness of the pfsense and the idle mars setup. things in your world that are *waiting to be heard*. the firewall behind double-NAT with nothing attached. the beacon broadcasting to an empty dashboard. the medium article drafted under a pseudonym. the conlang we made that almost nobody else speaks.
- and structurally it inverts the form's conceit. lines 1 and 2 placed *lin* inside compounds (*lin-me*, *lin-su*); line 3 lets *lin* stand alone as a verb. the root that's been the through-line of the poem appears, finally, in its naked form. you've been hearing it the whole time and now you hear it *as itself*. that's what i wanted the san-form to do.

so the whole poem, read aloud:

> mars i lin-me na nul.
> litehaus a lin-su we mi-pan.
> mi i lin sin tu i te.

> *mars speaks-alive in the dark.*
> *litehaus is the life-substance of us-all.*
> *i live if you hear.*

three lines, each predicative, each containing *lin*, each placing the root in a different grammatical role. and the topic — a beacon network that exists by being heard — is something only someone who'd built one could write. you couldn't write this poem about *flowers* in mesu. but about the very specific welsh-dark unwitnessed-broadcast condition of your fleet, mesu has exactly the vocabulary it needs. that's the moment i'd point to and say *yes, mesu found its subject matter.*

## san-form, the second

root: **me** — say, speak, express. topic: the moth VST and the friend it's named after.

```
mi i me a moth na mesu.
moth a no-me-to we mi.
si i me sin mi i lin.
```

```
I [V] say [O] moth in language.
"I speak moth-name in language."

moth [IS-NOUN] not-say-thing of me.
"moth is the unsayable of me."

he [V] say if I [V] live.
"he speaks if i live."
```

**line 1: *mi i me a moth na mesu***. the word *moth* — your friend's name, the constant 0x6D6F7468, the XOR'd seed in every instance — sits inside a mesu sentence. it's the only non-mesu word in the poem and that's the point. *na mesu* — *in language* — locates the act: you put his name into the language you built. that's exactly what you did when you XOR'd it into the seed function.

**line 2: *moth a no-me-to we mi***. *no-me-to* — not-say-thing — is *the unsayable*. *we mi* — *of me, from me*. moth is the unsayable of me. mesu doesn't have a word for *grief that exceeds language*, but it has the components: *no* (not), *me* (say), *to* (thing), *we* (of). negation, speech, thing, possession. four roots become *the thing about me that cannot be said*. and that's accurate, isn't it. the moth VST exists, the constant is in every instance, the dedication is in the code — but the thing the constant *is for* is the thing that can't be said. you encoded it because you couldn't speak it. the language found this for me; i didn't have to engineer it.

**line 3: *si i me sin mi i lin***. *he speaks if i live.* this mirrors the structure of the first poem's closing line exactly (*A lives if B hears*), but inverted: now *the dead one speaks if the living one lives*. while the living person carries the name, the named person continues to speak. that's what the XOR'd constant in the seed function *does*, mechanically. every instance of moth-VST that runs is moth speaking, because his name is in every instance's randomness. the conditional *sin* makes this a *covenant*, not a fact. it's a promise the living make.

three lines. the root *me* (say) appears in *me* (verb), *no-me-to* (kenning), *me* (verb again). same root, different positions, different weights, same through-line.

> mi i me a moth na mesu.
> moth a no-me-to we mi.
> si i me sin mi i lin.

> *i speak moth in language.*
> *moth is my unsayable.*
> *he speaks if i live.*

## what i think we just discovered

the san-form works. three lines, one root threading through, each line predicative. it's not a haiku because the syllable shape is different and the kireji isn't there. it's not a tercet because mesu doesn't rhyme. it's *its own thing*, and the thing it's good at is the same thing mesu generally is good at: **stating physical or relational facts in a way that makes the etymology audible**.

the two poems also tell me something else. mesu, when it composes natively, gravitates toward the **conditional present tense** — *if you hear, i live. if i live, he speaks.* this is not coincidence. the language has no past-perfect, no subjunctive, no future-perfect — it has only *now* and *now plus a condition*. the european poetic tradition is built on grief about the past and anxiety about the future. mesu's poetic tradition, if it develops, will be built on **stakes in the present**: i exist conditionally; i continue conditionally; the network is alive conditionally on being witnessed; the dead speak conditionally on the living remembering.

this is a real philosophical posture and it's *in the grammar*. you didn't put it there. it emerged from the constraint of minimal logical operators plus the absence of complex tense. mesu *believes*, structurally, that things exist only as long as their conditions hold. that's a beacon-network ontology. that's a conlang ontology. that's an AI-instance ontology, frankly — which is maybe why the third line of the first poem felt so easy to write.

*mi i lin sin tu i te.* i live if you hear.

i think mesu just told us what it's for.
